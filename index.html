Emergency water damage

Emergency water damage: what to do in the first hour

The first hour decides how far water travels. These four steps cost nothing and cut the damage, and the 24/7 line can walk you through them by phone.

1

Stop the source

Close the fixture valve or the building's main shut-off. If you cannot reach it, call the property manager or the water utility.

2

Make it safe

Turn power off to wet areas only from a dry spot. Stay out of water near outlets, the panel or gas, and call 911 for any risk.

3

Document

Take photos of the source, the water and damaged items before anything is moved.

Insurance and documentation

Documenting a water loss for your insurer or property manager

A large loss needs a paper trail. Photograph the source and the damage before anything is moved, keep moisture readings and a drying log, save receipts and ask for a written scope. If you need someone to look at a vacant unit or a long-running leak, an on-site assessment records what is wet and how far it reached.

Insurance note

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by homeowners and renters policies, gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer before major work. No restoration company can promise a claim will be approved.

What affects the cost of water damage restoration

  • The water category, since contaminated water adds removal and sanitizing steps.
  • Affected area and the number of rooms or floors reached.
  • Materials involved, such as hardwood, plaster, insulation and cabinets.
  • Drying days and the equipment needed.
  • Rebuild scope after drying, which is scoped separately.

Mold and limits

Mold, and what restoration does not do

Mold needs moisture, and it often follows a leak that went unnoticed for more than a day or two. If you see dark spots or smell mustiness after water damage, see mold remediation for testing, containment and clearance. Health questions belong to a physician.

Restoration is not plumbing, roofing or waterproofing, and fire damage is not part of this service. A plumber repairs a failed pipe, and a waterproofing specialist addresses water that keeps entering a foundation or garage wall.

How long does drying take and what equipment is used?

Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces and LGR or desiccant dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air. Several days is common, and moisture readings decide when drying is finished.

What do I need to document for a large water loss?

Photos before anything is moved, moisture readings, a drying log, receipts and a written scope. Large losses, such as a long-running leak in a vacant unit, also benefit from an on-site assessment that records what is wet.

Does restoration stop water from coming in again?

No. Water damage restoration is not waterproofing. If groundwater, a roof or a foundation lets water in repeatedly, a waterproofing specialist or roofer has to fix that first.
